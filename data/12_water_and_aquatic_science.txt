Water Science and Freshwater Basics

Water is made of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, with the formula H2O. Pure water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius and boils at 100 degrees Celsius at sea level. Seawater freezes at a lower temperature, about minus 1.8 degrees Celsius, because of the dissolved salt.

Only about 2.5 percent of Earth's water is fresh water, and most of that is locked in glaciers, ice caps and groundwater. Rivers and lakes hold a tiny fraction of the total. The water cycle moves water between the ocean, atmosphere and land through evaporation, condensation, precipitation and runoff.

Dissolved oxygen is the amount of oxygen gas in the water and is essential for fish. Cold water holds more dissolved oxygen than warm water. Water quality is also measured by pH, temperature, turbidity (cloudiness), salinity and nutrient levels.

Density of seawater increases with salinity and falls as temperature rises. Ice is less dense than liquid water, so it floats. Sea ice forms when ocean water freezes, and it reflects sunlight, helping keep polar regions cool. Melting sea ice does not change sea level much, because it already floats, but melting ice on land does.

Brackish water is a mix of fresh and salt water, found in estuaries. Desalination removes salt from seawater to make fresh water, using methods such as reverse osmosis and distillation, and is used in many dry coastal regions including parts of Tamil Nadu.

A monsoon is a seasonal wind reversal that brings heavy rain to South Asia. It is driven by the temperature difference between the land and the Indian Ocean.
