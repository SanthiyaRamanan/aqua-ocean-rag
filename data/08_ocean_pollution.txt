Ocean Pollution

Ocean pollution comes from many sources, and most of it begins on land. The main types include plastic waste, oil spills, agricultural runoff, sewage, chemical and industrial waste, noise and ghost fishing gear.

Millions of tonnes of plastic enter the ocean each year. Plastic does not fully biodegrade. Instead it breaks into smaller pieces called microplastics, which are smaller than five millimetres. Marine animals can swallow plastic or become entangled in it. Plastic accumulates in the centres of gyres, such as the Great Pacific Garbage Patch, which is more like a vast area of floating debris than a solid island.

Eutrophication happens when fertilizers and sewage add too much nitrogen and phosphorus to coastal water. This causes algal blooms. When the algae die and decompose, bacteria use up the oxygen, creating hypoxic areas known as dead zones. One of the largest is in the Gulf of Mexico near the Mississippi River outflow.

Oil spills, such as the Deepwater Horizon spill in 2010, coat wildlife, poison food webs and can damage habitats for years. Heavy metals such as mercury build up in the food chain through bioaccumulation and biomagnification, reaching high levels in top predators such as swordfish and tuna.

Underwater noise from ships, sonar and construction disturbs whales and dolphins that depend on sound to communicate and navigate.

Solutions include reducing single use plastics, better waste management, treating wastewater, cleaner shipping, international agreements such as MARPOL and the Global Plastics Treaty negotiations, and community beach clean-ups.
