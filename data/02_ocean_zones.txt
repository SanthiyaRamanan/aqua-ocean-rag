Ocean Zones and Layers

The ocean is divided into vertical zones based on how much sunlight reaches them. The epipelagic or sunlight zone extends from the surface to about 200 metres. Almost all photosynthesis happens here, and most familiar marine animals such as tuna, dolphins and sharks live in this layer.

The mesopelagic or twilight zone reaches from about 200 to 1,000 metres. Only faint light penetrates, so photosynthesis is not possible. Many animals here, such as lanternfish, use bioluminescence and migrate upward at night to feed. This daily movement is the largest animal migration on Earth.

The bathypelagic or midnight zone lies between about 1,000 and 4,000 metres. It is completely dark and the water is near freezing. Animals such as anglerfish and giant squid live in this zone and have adapted to high pressure and scarce food.

The abyssopelagic or abyssal zone extends from about 4,000 to 6,000 metres and covers much of the deep seafloor. Below that is the hadal zone, which includes the deep ocean trenches from about 6,000 metres to the bottom of the Mariana Trench.

Pressure increases by about one atmosphere for every 10 metres of depth. At the bottom of the Challenger Deep the pressure is over 1,000 times the pressure at the surface. Temperature falls quickly with depth through a layer called the thermocline, then stays close to 0 to 4 degrees Celsius in the deep sea.
