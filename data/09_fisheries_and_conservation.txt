Fisheries, Aquaculture and Marine Conservation

Fisheries provide food and income for billions of people. Overfishing happens when fish are caught faster than they can reproduce, causing populations to decline. Bycatch is the accidental capture of non-target species such as turtles, dolphins and seabirds. Bottom trawling can damage seafloor habitats.

Sustainable fishing methods include setting catch limits, protecting breeding grounds, using selective gear, enforcing seasonal closures and eco-labelling programs such as the Marine Stewardship Council label. In India, many states enforce a seasonal fishing ban during the monsoon months, typically around 61 days, to protect fish breeding.

Aquaculture is the farming of fish, shellfish and aquatic plants. It now supplies roughly half of the fish that people eat. Examples include shrimp farming, salmon pens, oyster farming and seaweed cultivation. Poorly managed aquaculture can cause pollution, disease and loss of mangroves.

A marine protected area, or MPA, is a section of ocean where human activity is limited to protect nature. Well-designed and enforced MPAs allow fish populations to recover and can also benefit nearby fisheries through spillover. Only a small percentage of the global ocean is currently fully protected.

The 30 by 30 target, adopted in the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework in 2022, aims to protect at least 30 percent of the world's ocean and land by 2030.

Endangered marine species include the blue whale, hawksbill turtle, vaquita porpoise and many shark and ray species. Marine mammals are protected by laws and treaties, and the International Whaling Commission regulates whaling.
