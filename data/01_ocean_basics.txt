Ocean Basics

Earth has one connected global ocean, traditionally divided into five named basins: the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern (Antarctic) and Arctic Oceans. The ocean covers about 71 percent of Earth's surface and holds roughly 97 percent of the planet's water. The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest, covering about one third of the globe.

The average depth of the ocean is about 3,700 metres. The deepest known point is the Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench in the western Pacific, at nearly 11,000 metres below sea level. Most of the ocean floor has been mapped only at low resolution compared with the surface of the Moon or Mars.

Seawater is salty because rivers and rocks add dissolved minerals over millions of years. The average salinity is about 35 grams of salt per kilogram of water, written as 35 parts per thousand. Sodium and chloride are the most abundant ions. Salinity is lower near river mouths and melting ice and higher in hot, dry regions where evaporation is strong, such as the Red Sea.

The ocean produces more than half of the oxygen we breathe, mostly through tiny photosynthetic plankton called phytoplankton. It also absorbs a large share of the carbon dioxide and heat produced by human activity, which makes it a major climate regulator.

A sea is generally a smaller part of the ocean that is partly enclosed by land, such as the Mediterranean Sea, Arabian Sea or Bay of Bengal. A gulf or bay is a part of the sea that cuts into the land.
